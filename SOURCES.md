## Choices and limits

- **MyCOL match types.** `is_mycol` is `yes` when the role title appears in an occupation's "job titles included" list, `near` when we matched the closest MyCOL occupation or a similar title, and `no` when MyCOL has nothing close. `near` rows are shown as "based on a related occupation". The MyCOL PDF's occupation titles and MASCO codes are images; we read them with OCR and checked the matches by hand, so re-check any code before relying on it. The `mycol_source_*` columns in `roles.csv` hold this source.
- **IT areas.** The 5 areas (`subfield_code` in `roles.csv`) are the team's own grouping of 25 O*NET IT occupations. The RIASEC scores in `onet/role_onet.csv` are O*NET data.
- **Importance filter.** `role_skills.csv` keeps O*NET skill and knowledge ratings with importance of 3.0 or higher. A skill missing from a role therefore means "below 3.0 or not rated". Importance ratings are kept as published (none of the kept rows is flagged "Recommend Suppress"). Seven level values that O*NET flags "Recommend Suppress" are left blank, so those seven rows have an importance but no level. The matching code skips them.
- **Technology rows.** `row_type = in_demand_tech` rows in `role_skills.csv` have no importance or level, because O*NET gives technologies only an In Demand flag. We did not estimate numbers. `is_in_demand_tech = Y` in `skills.csv` means O*NET's In Demand flag, which is stricter than O*NET's Hot Technology flag.
- **Mathematics** appears twice, as a skill and as a knowledge area, because O*NET lists it under two element IDs.
- **SDG links** are mostly `indirect`. Many general skills map to target 4.4 as general employability skills.
- **Education.** Job Zone is O*NET's U.S. measure of preparation. It is not a Malaysian qualification rule. Roles above the user's level are ranked later and marked "needs extra education", not hidden.
- **Area fit.** `0.45 × skills test + 0.30 × preference + 0.25 × CV and tools`, per IT area. CV and tools = the higher of the CV evidence score (next point) and the tools score: how many of the area's 10 most-listed O*NET In Demand technologies the student uses (found in the CV or ticked on the Skills page), each weighted by the share of the area's roles that list it; every area is measured against 10 tools, so areas with long tool lists are not penalised. The higher one counts, not the sum, because both describe the same experience. Preference = `0.6 × the area's 4 questionnaire items + 0.4 × the average RIASEC fit of the area's roles`; the items weigh more because the O*NET interest profiles of software, data, security and networking roles correlate 0.85–0.98, so RIASEC alone hardly separates them. A part the student skipped is left out, never filled. See `scripts/matching.py`.
- **CV evidence per IT area.** When the LLM is available, it lists evidence items from the uploaded CV: an IT area, a kind and a quote copied from the CV. Our code keeps an item only if its quote (at least 12 characters) appears in the CV text, ignoring case, spacing, curly quotes and dash types, and the area and kind are known; the page says how many were left out. Our code, not the LLM, then scores each area: coursework 10 points, a personal project, competition or certification 20, an internship or job 35, added up and capped at 100; an area with no evidence scores 0. These points are team choices, not data: work done for an employer says more than a project or certificate the student chose, which says more than coursework every student in the course does (`EVIDENCE_POINTS` in `scripts/cv_reader.py`). Without the LLM (no key, failed call, rejected reply or no quote found), the CV's area scores come from the technologies it names, as in the tools score above, and `cv_areas_method` says which method was used. The LLM decides which area and kind a quote shows, and that judgement is not checked; the quote check only stops invented experience. The browser keeps the six area scores for the matches page, never the CV text or the quotes.
- **Student flow and focused skills test.** The steps run CV (optional; self-ratings instead) → interests → skills test → results, because students who finished the questionnaire gave up on the full 24-question test. The test now checks only the areas the first two steps point to (`choose_areas` in `scripts/assessment.py`): CV areas whose "CV and tools" score is 50 or more (at most 3, highest first) plus the top 3 interest areas by preference score. Fewer than 3: add the next-highest interest areas, then the next-highest CV areas; no CV and no usable interests: all 5. So 3 to 5 areas, each with the same 3 quick-check items, then deep and hands-on items in the 2 strongest tested areas, as before: 3 areas = 15 questions, 5 areas = 21. The server refuses an area list that is not 3–5 distinct area ids, and the list travels with the seed in every call (next, result, the match re-grading and share links); a request without one is the full 5-area test, so older share links still work. An area not tested has **no** test score, never a filled-in one: area fit and skill use its CV evidence only. The 50 threshold and the counts 3/3/3–5 are team choices. The time shown before the test is the mean `time_s` per stage of the bank's items in those areas; the hands-on items dominate (about 5.5 minutes each), so 3 areas still take about 25 minutes.
- **Testing the other areas later.** After the result, the student may add any untested area ("Want to test your other IT areas?"): each gets its own 3 quick-check items, picked from a random stream per area and seed, so earlier questions never change and adding areas one at a time or together asks the same items. The added list travels as `added` next to `areas` (result, match re-grading, share links); it must name untested areas, each once. The deep stage is not re-run: the 2 areas tested in depth stay those of the focused test, and an added area is scored like any quick-check-only area (never above the weaker in-depth area). The result groups are then worked out again with the new scores. The minutes shown per area are the mean `time_s` of its quick-check items × 3.
- **Result groups.** Each area gets an interest score (its preference part) and a skill score (its skills-test and CV-and-tools parts, weighted 45:25 as in area fit, missing parts left out). "High" = among the student's top 3 areas on that score, above 0; ties go to the higher area fit. High interest and high skill = "Best fit for you": its roles are listed first. High skill only = "You can do this, but it may not excite you": its roles follow. High interest only = "Want to get there?": not in the recommended list; shown with the 5 biggest skill gaps across its 3 best-matching roles and links to their learning paths. Neither = not shown (listed under "Not shown" with the reason). Within a group roles keep the match-score order. With no skill evidence (no test, CV or tools) the top 3 interest areas are shown as "skills not checked yet", with a button to the test; with no interests, the top 3 skill areas as "interests not checked yet". See `area_groups` in `scripts/matching.py`.
- **Score ranges.** Each skills-test score comes from 3 questions, so the results page also shows a likely range: the 80% Wilson interval of the share of points earned, with the effective number of questions `(Σ points)² / Σ points²` (3 for a quick check; 2.7 for the 2-2-4 point in-depth set). One right answer more or less moves a quick-check score by 33 points. The 80% level is a team choice. Ranges are shown only; matching uses the score itself (`score_range` in `scripts/assessment.py`).
- **What backs the questions.** The results page states how many questions cite a source for their answer and match a certification exam section, and, once done, how many outside experts reviewed the bank and how many students piloted it (`review` in `data/it_assessment.json`, both 0 on 2026-10-31). No expert review or pilot has been done yet, so the page says so.
- **Skills test score per area.** An area tested in depth scores `0.4 × quick check + 0.6 × readiness`. An area seen only in the quick check keeps its quick-check score but never more than the weaker of the two areas tested in depth: it scored no higher in the quick check, and 3 easy items say less than harder ones. The test result page and the matches use this same score, so they name the same best area (`scripts/assessment.py`).
- **Skills shown by the test.** Each question is tagged with the O*NET skills and knowledge it tests. A right answer counts as level 3 (quick check), 4 (in depth) or 5 (hands-on) on the O*NET 0–7 scale in those skills, times the share of points earned; the best answer per skill counts, and a wrong answer lowers nothing. The student's own rating is used when it is higher. The three levels are team choices read off O*NET's level anchors.
- **Role score.** `0.4 × area fit + 0.1 × interest + 0.3 × skill + 0.2 × trend`; skill = 70% rated skills + 30% In Demand technologies. Interest is the Pearson correlation between the student's and the role's RIASEC scores. All weights are team design choices, not data.
- **Trend score.** Member A's signals (`trend_signals.csv`) give a strength from −1 (falling) to +1 (rising) and a confidence. `scripts/trend_score.py` turns each part into `50 + 50 × the confidence-weighted average strength` (high 1, medium 2/3, low 1/3). Malaysian demand: the share of the area's roles on MyCOL (yes 1, near 0.5, no 0), DOSM ads (+1 / 0 / −1 by threshold) and, for a role, its own MyCOL status. AI momentum: arXiv AI paper growth divided by 40%, capped at −1 and +1, so +40% or more counts in full. Trend = 50/50 of the two parts; a part with no data counts as 50 (flat). The half credit for "near" and the 40% are team choices.
- **Future scenarios.** The three scenarios (`SCENARIOS` in `matching.py`) only re-weight the two measured parts of each trend score: current trends 50/50, fast AI adoption 20% demand / 80% AI momentum, Malaysian demand leads 80/20. They change no data, and the weights are team assumptions.
- **Learning path page.** For each role: free resources for its IT area, resources made for the role, then its skills and tools with resources (technologies first), then certifications. Resources come only from `learning_resources.csv` and `courses.csv`; a skill with no checked resource says so. Salaries are not shown: we found no public Malaysian salary data per IT role (DOSM publishes only MASCO major-group medians).
- **Learning path.** Gaps are ordered by importance × missing level, then missing technologies, then skills the student has not rated or shown in the skills test: those count as 0, so they always look like big gaps, and putting them last keeps unasked general skills (reading, listening) from crowding out the technical ones. Gaps smaller than 0.5 O*NET level points get no course. Total time = sum of stated course hours; weeks = hours ÷ the student's study hours per week. Courses with no stated length are counted separately.
- **Empty fields.** `ai_replacement_risk` in `roles.csv` is empty until we have a sourced figure. We do not fill it from opinion.
- **Data policy.** Only public or synthetic data. No real CVs or personal data.

## AI model in the product

- **gpt-oss-120b** on **Fireworks AI** (`accounts/fireworks/models/gpt-oss-120b`), open-weight model under Apache 2.0, called through the Fireworks chat completions API. Used only to word the "Explain this for me" text from facts our code computed, and to read an uploaded CV: a summary, and evidence items whose quotes our code checks against the CV before scoring them (`scripts/cv_reader.py`). Replies with numbers not in those facts or in the CV are rejected (`scripts/explain.py`). It is not a data source.

## Known risks and fallbacks

- **Hardest risk: almost no role-level Malaysian demand data.** All IT roles sit in one DOSM industry (Information and communication), and DOSM publishes online ads for only one IT occupation (Software Developers). MyCOL is the only official Malaysian source that separates IT roles, and it is one edition.
- **Fallback.** (1) A missing number is never estimated: the app shows "no data", and in the trend score that part counts as 50 (flat), so an area is not ranked higher only because a part could not be measured. (2) Every data point keeps its source, release date and fetch date, and the Update data job keeps the last good rows when a source fails. (3) Next step: read the vacancy and shortage icons on the MyMahir occupation pages, and add the next DOSM quarter (2026 Q3 release, due 2026-11-17).
- **Second risk: course coverage.** `courses.csv` covers 26 of the 196 skills the roles use. The app says "No verified free course in our list yet" for the rest instead of suggesting an unchecked one.
